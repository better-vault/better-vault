A plugin adds something to better-vault: a Studio tool of its own, a smaller panel, a mod that reshapes the whole app, or a colour theme.

## Install a plugin

<Steps>

### Find it

Open **Settings → Plugins** and choose **Browse**. The directory lists every plugin that was reviewed and merged into [better-vault-plugins](https://github.com/better-vault/better-vault-plugins); search it by name, author or tag. You can also browse it on [better-vault.dev/plugins](/plugins).

### Read the details

Choose a plugin to open its dialog. It says what the plugin adds and what it asks to read or change, under a warning that it is a third-party plugin. Choose **Install…** when you are ready.

### Confirm

The confirm step shows the warning and the permissions again. Untick anything you don't want to give it, decide whether to turn it on now, and choose **Install**.

</Steps>

To install a plugin someone sent you as a file, choose **Install** in the **Installed** tab and pick its `.bvplugin.json` file. It goes through the same dialog, marked as unverified. An AI agent can also build one for you; [Creator mode](/docs/creator-mode) explains how.

## Turn it off or uninstall it

Choose an installed plugin to open its dialog again. The switch turns it on or off; **Uninstall** removes it with everything it saved. Its Studio tools, panels, mods and theme go at once, and the app looks and works as it did before.

## What a plugin can use

| Permission                  | Lets the plugin                                          |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Read your notebooks**     | List your notebooks and how many sources each has        |
| **Read notebooks' sources** | List a notebook's sources and read their text            |
| **Read your notes**         | List your notes and read them                            |
| **Read your skills**        | Read your skills and their instructions                  |
| **Read your calendar**      | List your events between two days                        |
| **Create notes**            | Create notes in the current vault                        |
| **Add calendar events**     | Add events to your calendar                              |
| **Use your AI model**       | Ask the model you chose, with your key, which it never sees |

A plugin never sees your settings or API keys, whatever it asks for. Every plugin can also keep a little data of its own, such as scores or preferences, on your device.

## How plugins are kept safe

A plugin's pages run in a sandbox. They can't reach any server, so they can't load scripts or pictures from the internet or send your data anywhere, and they can't read the app's storage or other pages. The only way a page gets your data is by asking better-vault, which answers only what you allowed. If a page tries to leave, better-vault stops it.

A mod is CSS only. It is checked before it installs and again before it is applied: it may not import anything or load anything but `data:` pictures and fonts, and the app's Content-Security-Policy blocks other hosts as well. A theme can only set the app's colour tokens to plain colours.

When a plugin is updated and asks for more than before, it turns off until you look at it again. Plugins in a backup, or synced from another device, also arrive turned off.
